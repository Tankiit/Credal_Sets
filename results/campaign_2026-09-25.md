## 2. Supervision ablation (λ_a = 0)

Without ambiguity supervision, **the AU head collapses to a constant in all 3 seeds**, so ρ(EU, AU) and ρ(AU, H) are undefined. Task accuracy (77.4%) and EU (AUROC 0.72) are unaffected. A non-trivial ambiguity score therefore comes entirely from its supervision.

## 3. Matched decorrelation penalty (λ_d = 5)

**The penalty collapses one of the two uncertainty heads in every seed:**
- the EU head in seeds 42 and 2024;
- the AU head in seed 123.

Accuracy is unchanged (77.2%). The penalty reaches low correlation by destroying a signal, not by separating two informative ones. This matches the gradient audit (`gradient_audit_with_decorr5.json`), where the decorrelation term is the only one with a path to both uncertainty pathways, and at λ_d = 5 it dominates both heads' gradients.

- **Against the standard epistemic baselines:** CBM EU matches ensemble mutual information on DistilBERT and beats it on RoBERTa. It is within the MC-dropout range.
- **Against confidence:** confidence-based scores (MaxProb, entropy) remain the best error detectors.
- **Not included:** Semantic Entropy and P(True) apply only to LLM generations and are not used.

## Consequences for the paper

- **Table 1:** replace the CEBaB rows with the retrained 3-seed values above and drop "provisional" (TODO-e resolved by retraining). Add seed 42 to every row, which meets the n = 3 rebuttal promise.
- **Abstract:** the penalty-free low-correlation claim holds on CEBaB only. HateXplain and GoEmotions have constant AU heads, and the QA models use a penalty.
- **New results to report:**
  - AU needs its supervision.
  - A decorrelation penalty collapses a head rather than separating the signals.
  - CBM EU is on par with ensemble and MC-dropout epistemic baselines but below confidence.
